\# TARDIS Den

\# **Narrative Environment Specification**



Version: 1.0



Status: Draft



Author: Om Kagilkar



\---



\# Purpose



This document defines the emotional identity of TARDIS Den.



It does not describe features.



It describes meaning.



Every object, animation, sound, and environmental change should reinforce these principles.



If a new feature contradicts this document, the feature should be redesigned before implementation.



\---



\# Core Identity



TARDIS Den is a personal sanctuary.



It is not a game.



It is not a simulation.



It is not a productivity tool.



It is a room that exists to preserve curiosity, memories, and quiet moments.



The room reflects its owner rather than demanding attention.



Nothing inside the room should ever compete for attention.



Everything waits patiently until interacted with.



\---



\# The Room is Alive



The room should never feel static.



However, it should never feel busy.



Movement exists only where movement naturally belongs.



Examples:



Stars slowly drift.



Dust particles float through light.



Curtains move gently.



The computer screen softly glows.



The telescope lens reflects distant light.



The journal pages settle after opening.



Nothing moves simply to attract attention.



\---



\# Time Exists



Time matters.



The room remembers.



Examples:



Journal pages accumulate.



Photographs increase.



Books remain bookmarked.



Favorites remain on the desk.



Recently explored planets appear in the telescope history.



Calendar dates change naturally.



The room slowly becomes more personal.



\---



\# Knowledge Leaves Marks



Learning should leave visible traces.



Examples:



Books gain bookmarks.



Sticky notes appear.



Highlighted passages remain.



Frequently opened books become visually worn.



The telescope remembers favorite observations.



Knowledge becomes part of the room.



\---



\# Memories Become Furniture



Memories should never be hidden inside folders.



Instead, they become physical objects.



Examples:



Polaroids appear clipped to a string.



Favorite photographs stand inside frames.



Journal pages naturally become thicker.



A postcard from a memorable day remains on the desk.



Objects should celebrate memories instead of storing them.



\---



\# Technology Should Feel Invisible



Technology powers the room.



It should never dominate it.



The administrator should forget that APIs, databases, and cloud storage exist.



Interactions should feel physical.



Examples:



Instead of clicking Upload,



Place the photo inside the journal.



Instead of opening Settings,



Sit at the computer.



Instead of navigating folders,



Look through photographs.



\---



\# Curiosity is Rewarded



The room should encourage exploration.



Examples:



Looking through the telescope reveals discoveries.



Opening books uncovers unexpected facts.



A meteor shower occasionally appears outside.



Special astronomical events become visible.



The room rewards attention rather than speed.



\---



\# Silence is Valuable



The room should never overwhelm.



Silence is part of the experience.



Animations should breathe.



Music should remain subtle.



Notifications should be rare.



There are no pop-ups demanding action.



The room waits.



\---



\# Personal Objects



Every personal object should feel earned.



Examples:



Journal pages.



Polaroids.



Bookmarks.



Favorite books.



Handwritten notes.



Desk decorations.



Nothing personal appears automatically.



The owner creates the room over time.



\---



\# Environmental Growth



The room evolves with usage.



Examples:



After 10 journal entries



A new bookmark appears.



After 50 entries



The journal becomes visibly thicker.



After 100 entries



The bookshelf gains a small commemorative object.



After a year



Seasonal decorations may appear.



Growth should feel natural.



Never gamified.



No achievement banners.



No confetti.



No reward popups.



\---



\# Weather Outside



The room exists beyond Earth.



The outside world is space.



Visible events may include:



Moving stars.



Nebulae.



Passing satellites.



The ISS.



Comets.



Meteor showers.



Planetary alignments.



Auroras.



Solar eclipses.



These events occur naturally.



They are not scheduled rewards.



\---



\# Emotional Palette



Every interaction should reinforce one or more of these feelings.



Wonder



Calm



Reflection



Curiosity



Comfort



Discovery



Ownership



Creativity



If a feature introduces stress, urgency, or unnecessary complexity, reconsider its implementation.



\---



\# Things the Room Never Does



Never flashes.



Never advertises.



Never pressures the owner.



Never interrupts.



Never forces engagement.



Never hides information behind unnecessary interactions.



Never becomes cluttered.



\---



\# Design Test



Before adding any feature, ask:



Would this object naturally exist in a real observatory-bedroom?



Would this interaction feel believable if this room existed physically?



Does this make the room feel more personal?



Does this encourage curiosity?



Does it preserve calmness?



If the answer is "no" to any of these, redesign the feature.



\---



\# Final Principle



The administrator should never feel like they are managing software.



They should feel like they have quietly entered a room that remembers them.
